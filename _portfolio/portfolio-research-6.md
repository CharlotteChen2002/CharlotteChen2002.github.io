<span style='font-size: 0.9em;'><em>M.S. Thesis, Columbia University</em>, Sept. 2026 – Present</span><br/>
<span style='font-size: 0.9em;'>This thesis studies how often an AI agent's microarchitectural proposals (e.g., ROB/LSQ sizing, new bypass paths, store-to-load forwarding) keep their simulated gains through RTL implementation, verification, and timing closure. Charlotte is building the evaluation environment on her 2-way out-of-order RISC-V core: an open Yosys/OpenROAD flow calibrated against Genus/Innovus, tiered verification (ISA-model co-simulation, hidden random instruction streams, JasperGold invariant proofs) with mutation testing, and an evaluate(patch) → JSON harness called by a collaborator's agent loop. A gem5 reference model lets the work compare a prediction-committing agent against simulator-only and RTL-coding agents on performance at achieved frequency per unit area.</span>
